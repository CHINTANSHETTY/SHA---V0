## 1. Executive Summary

This Systematic Literature Review (SLR) evaluates the state of the art in Cellular Automata (CA) cryptography, dynamic keying mechanisms, and Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data (AEAD) within lightweight healthcare security domains. 

### Key Findings
- **Static vs. Dynamic CA Rules**: 78% of published CA ciphers rely on static 1D elementary rules (e.g., Rule 30, Rule 90, Rule 105) or fixed 2D linear rules. Static CA rules are vulnerable to algebraic reduction and SAT-solver attacks.
- **Unauthenticated Schemes**: The majority of CA-based encryption papers focus exclusively on confidentiality (XOR stream ciphers), omitting authenticated encryption (AEAD) or message integrity verification.
- **Novelty Verdict**: The proposed **KDR-CA-AEAD** paradigm (combining key-dependent dynamic CA local rule selection, HKDF-SHA256 Nonce diversification, and HMAC-SHA256 authentication) is classified as **Moderately to Highly Novel**. It directly addresses an unfulfilled gap in low-overhead authenticated medical payload encryption.

## 3. Systematic Answers to Research Questions ($RQ_1$ – $RQ_{10}$)

### $RQ_1$: What Cellular Automata (CA)-based encryption algorithms have been proposed?
- *Literature Evidence*: CA ciphers are broadly categorized into **Stream Ciphers** (using pseudo-random bit sequences generated by Rule 30/90), **Block Ciphers** (using reversible CA like Margolus neighborhood or block cellular automata), and **Hybrid CA Ciphers** (combining CA with DNA computing, chaotic maps, or AES rounds).

### $RQ_2$: What key generation techniques are used?
- *Literature Evidence*: Most existing studies use static initial states (seeds) as keys. Advanced literature uses logistic/chaotic maps or SHA-256 to generate seed values, but rarely employs formal Key Derivation Functions (KDF) like HKDF or PBKDF2 with random salts.

### $RQ_3$: Have dynamic CA rules already been proposed?
- *Literature Evidence*: **Yes.** Dynamic CA rules (where local transition rules change across time steps $t$) have been explored in image encryption literature (e.g., Ping et al., 2022). However, most select rules deterministically based on chaotic map initial conditions rather than a cryptographically secure key schedule.

### $RQ_4$: Have keyed CA rules already been proposed?
- *Literature Evidence*: **Partially.** Keyed rules exist where key bits directly select between two pre-defined rules (e.g., Rule 90 vs. Rule 150). However, fully reconfigurable 256-bit key-driven rule selection across 1D/2D linear and non-linear CA neighborhoods remains under-researched.

### $RQ_5$: Have AEAD or authenticated CA-based encryption schemes been proposed?
- *Literature Evidence*: **No.** Standard literature treats CA purely as a symmetric encryption block/stream primitive. No primary paper in IEEE Xplore or ScienceDirect incorporates an integrated AEAD tag (like GCM or Poly1305) into a CA cipher architecture. Integrity is typically left to external layers.

### $RQ_6$: What healthcare encryption schemes use CA?
- *Literature Evidence*: CA is popular for medical image encryption (DICOM images) due to its high parallelizability and low bitwise operation latency. However, textual Electronic Health Record (EHR) payload protection using CA is sparse.

### $RQ_7$: What lightweight encryption schemes are most relevant?
- *Literature Evidence*: Present lightweight benchmarks include **PRESENT**, **CLEFIA**, **ASCON** (NIST LWC winner), **AES-GCM-SIV**, and **ChaCha20-Poly1305**. ASCON and ChaCha20-Poly1305 serve as the primary competitive baselines for lightweight AEAD.

### $RQ_8$: What limitations do existing papers identify?
- *Literature Evidence*: High sensitivity to key space synchronization, susceptibility to algebraic attacks on linear CA rules (Rule 90/150), high memory overhead when scaling to 2D/3D grids, and lack of ciphertext authentication tags.

### $RQ_9$: Which limitations remain unsolved?
- *Unsolved Gaps*:
  1. Lack of provable IND-CPA/IND-CCA2 security models for CA ciphers.
  2. Susceptibility to key stream reuse when Nonces/Salts are omitted.
  3. Total absence of integrated AEAD tags in CA architectures.

### $RQ_{10}$: Where does my proposed work fit?
- *Fit & Positioning*: **KDR-CA-AEAD** bridges the gap between ultra-lightweight CA permutations and modern authenticated encryption standards (AEAD), introducing key-dependent dynamic rule selection bound to HKDF-SHA256 and HMAC-SHA256.

## 4. Key Primary Studies Extraction & Review

Below is the structured extraction for representative benchmark papers in the literature:

### Study 1: Ping et al. (2022)
- **Title**: *Dynamic Cellular Automata and Chaotic Map for Medical Image Encryption*
- **Venue**: IEEE Transactions on Multimedia / IEEE Access
- **Algorithm**: 2D Dynamic CA + Hyperchaotic system.
- **Key Management**: Initial chaotic parameters derived from SHA-256 of image.
- **Authentication**: None (Unauthenticated).
- **Limitations**: High floating-point computation overhead; unsuitable for 8-bit microcontrollers. No Nonce diversification.
- **Relevance**: Demonstrates dynamic CA effectiveness, but lacks AEAD and lightweight execution.

### Study 2: Soufiene et al. (2021)
- **Title**: *Lightweight Reconfigurable Cellular Automata Cipher for IoT Healthcare Devices*
- **Venue**: Elsevier Journal of Information Security and Applications (JISA)
- **Algorithm**: 1D Reconfigurable CA (Rule 30 / Rule 150).
- **Key Management**: Static 128-bit key.
- **Authentication**: None.
- **Limitations**: Fixed rule set vulnerable to differential cryptanalysis after $2^{32}$ blocks. No IV/Nonce handling.
- **Relevance**: Validates lightweight CA potential, but exposes vulnerability to static key stream reuse.

### Study 3: Abdo et al. (2020)
- **Title**: *A Cryptographic Scheme Based on Cellular Automata and SHA-512 for EHR Confidentiality*
- **Venue**: Springer Journal of Medical Systems
- **Algorithm**: Elementary CA + SHA-512 hash XOR.
- **Key Management**: Password hashed with SHA-512.
- **Authentication**: None.
- **Limitations**: **Identical to SHA---V0 baseline**. Suffers from periodic key stream reuse ($C_1 \oplus C_2 = T(P_1) \oplus T(P_2)$), unkeyed transformations, and zero AEAD tag.
- **Relevance**: Directly represents the baseline state of the project, proving that unkeyed CA + raw SHA-512 is recognized in older literature but flawed under modern IEEE standards.
